## Abstract

The advancement of modern technology increasingly depends on collaboration between different scientific disciplines. Nuclear science, traditionally associated with atomic physics and energy production, has become an important foundation for numerous applications in healthcare, agriculture, industrial engineering, environmental research, and space exploration.

This article explores the relationship between nuclear science and computational disciplines, particularly Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Computer Vision, and Data Science.

From the perspective of a Computer Science undergraduate student at the University of Lampung, it discusses how computational approaches can contribute to scientific analysis, technological innovation, and the development of emerging interdisciplinary applications.

This exploration was further encouraged by participation in the Global Atomic Quiz 2026, organized by Rosatom, which introduced a wide range of practical applications of nuclear technology in everyday life.

**Keywords:** Nuclear Science, Computer Science, Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Computer Vision, Data Science, Scientific Computing, Interdisciplinary Technology.

## 1. Introduction: Science Beyond Academic Boundaries

Some of the most important technological developments emerge when different scientific disciplines work together.

Computer Science has become an essential part of modern scientific research, providing tools for data processing, computational modeling, intelligent systems, and automated analysis.

At the same time, nuclear science continues to contribute to technologies that influence everyday life, from medical diagnostics and industrial inspection to energy generation and space exploration.

As a Computer Science undergraduate student, I find the relationship between these fields particularly interesting.

My academic interests are centered on Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Data Science, and Computer Vision. Exploring nuclear science provides another perspective on how these computational disciplines can interact with fundamental scientific knowledge.

## 2. Understanding the Everyday Applications of Nuclear Science

Nuclear technologies are not limited to nuclear power plants. Their applications can be found in several areas of modern society.

### 2.1 Precision Timekeeping and Connected Systems

Accurate time synchronization is essential for modern digital infrastructure.

Telecommunication networks, satellite navigation, financial systems, and distributed computing environments depend on reliable time references.

Atomic clocks provide this precision through the stable frequency characteristics of atoms. The international definition of the second is based on the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency of 9,192,631,770 hertz.

This is an interesting example of how fundamental physics supports the digital systems that modern society relies on.

### 2.2 Food Security and Agricultural Innovation

Nuclear techniques have several applications in agriculture and food production.

Food irradiation can reduce microorganisms and insect pests, helping preserve certain food products and improve food safety.

The Sterile Insect Technique is another application. It involves sterilizing selected insects through radiation before releasing them into targeted populations, helping reduce their reproduction.

These technologies demonstrate how nuclear science can support agricultural productivity and sustainable food systems.

### 2.3 Industrial Inspection and Material Development

Industrial applications provide another important example of nuclear technology in practice.

Radiographic testing uses X-rays or gamma rays to examine the internal structure of materials without damaging the inspected components.

Radiation processing can also improve the properties of selected polymer materials through molecular cross-linking.

These techniques contribute to manufacturing quality, infrastructure reliability, and industrial innovation.

## 3. Computational Intelligence in Scientific Applications

The increasing complexity of scientific instruments and engineering systems creates opportunities for computational approaches.

Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and Data Science can support the analysis of information generated by scientific and industrial processes.

### 3.1 Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

Machine learning methods can identify patterns in large datasets and assist with complex analytical tasks.

Potential applications in scientific and nuclear-related environments include:

- Equipment condition monitoring.
- Predictive maintenance.
- Anomaly detection.
- Scientific data interpretation.
- Energy system forecasting.
- Computational decision support.

These applications require appropriate validation, especially when used in environments involving complex physical systems and safety requirements.

### 3.2 Computer Vision and Image Analysis

Computer Vision has particularly interesting connections to scientific instrumentation.

Radiographic inspection, for example, produces images that can reveal internal defects in industrial materials.

Medical imaging also relies on image acquisition, reconstruction, and computational interpretation.

Computer Vision techniques may contribute to:

- Image classification and segmentation.
- Automated defect detection.
- Medical image analysis.
- Scientific image interpretation.
- Industrial visual inspection.

This field is particularly relevant to my academic interests because my undergraduate thesis focuses on Computer Vision.

Exploring these applications has strengthened my interest in understanding how image-processing methods can contribute to technological developments outside conventional software applications.

### 3.3 Data Science and Scientific Discovery

Scientific research increasingly generates complex datasets that require systematic analysis.

Data Science provides methods for extracting meaningful information from these datasets through statistical analysis, visualization, and computational modeling.

In nuclear-related research, these approaches may support environmental monitoring, radiation measurement analysis, experimental data processing, and energy forecasting.

The combination of computational knowledge and scientific understanding creates opportunities for interdisciplinary research.

## 4. Nuclear Energy and the Growing Digital World

The expansion of artificial intelligence, cloud computing, and digital infrastructure has increased the importance of reliable electricity supplies.

Data centers require substantial energy for servers, cooling infrastructure, storage systems, and networking equipment.

Nuclear energy offers one potential source of stable, low-carbon electricity that can contribute to meeting these requirements.

This relationship highlights an important point: the future of computing depends not only on algorithms, processors, and software architectures, but also on the physical infrastructure and energy systems supporting them.

Understanding this connection makes nuclear technology an interesting subject for students of Computer Science.

## 5. Nuclear Technology in Space Exploration

Nuclear science also plays an important role in space exploration.

Radioisotope thermoelectric generators convert heat from radioactive decay into electricity. These systems have supported spacecraft operating in environments where solar energy is limited.

Another important area is nuclear fusion.

Fusion is the process responsible for the energy produced by stars. Scientists are investigating controlled fusion as a potential future source of low-carbon energy.

Achieving controlled fusion requires extremely high plasma temperatures, sufficient density, and adequate energy confinement time.

These developments demonstrate the connection between fundamental physics, advanced engineering, and humanity's exploration of space.

## 7. Conclusion

The relationship between Computer Science and nuclear science demonstrates how different academic disciplines can complement one another.

Nuclear technologies contribute to healthcare, agriculture, industrial engineering, energy systems, environmental research, and space exploration.

Meanwhile, computational disciplines provide methods for analyzing scientific data, interpreting images, identifying patterns, and supporting technological development.

As a Computer Science undergraduate student, I believe that understanding developments outside one's primary field of study is an important part of academic growth.

Exploring nuclear science has broadened my perspective on the applications of technology and strengthened my appreciation for interdisciplinary scientific collaboration.
